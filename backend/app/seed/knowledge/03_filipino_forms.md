# Traditional Filipino Poetry Forms

These descriptions summarize commonly taught conventions. Historical practice varied by region and period, so AI-KEN distinguishes the traditional convention from modern adaptations and never labels a poem "traditional" unless the checks pass.

## Tanaga
- Structure: 4 lines (taludtod), 7 syllables (pantig) each — 28 syllables in total.
- Rhyme: traditionally monorhyme AAAA; modern tanaga writers also use AABB, ABAB, or ABBA.
- Character: compact, aphoristic, rich in talinghaga (metaphor). Pre-colonial Tagalog form revived by 20th-century poets.
- Example (7-7-7-7, AAAA tugma sa patinig):
  Ang puso'y parang ilog (ang-pu-so'y-pa-rang-i-log = 7)
  Sa luha'y umaagos
  Ngunit sa bawat agos
  May pag-asang susunod
  (Tugma: "ilog / umaagos / agos / susunod" — same final vowel o/u and final consonants from the malakas group g, s, d.)

## Diona
- Structure: commonly 3 lines of 7 syllables each.
- Rhyme: the same end rhyme on all lines (AAA).
- Use: courtship, wedding songs, short wisdom verses.

## Dalit
- Structure: commonly quatrains of 8 syllables per line (wawaluhin), monorhyme within the stanza.
- Use: devotional hymns, praise, prayer and reflective verse. The word has also been used for religious songs in general, so usage varies.

## Awit
- Structure: 4-line stanzas, 12 syllables per line (lalabindalawahin), caesura after the 6th syllable, monorhyme within each stanza (AAAA).
- Character: metrical romance/narrative, sung or chanted slowly (andante). Classic example: "Florante at Laura" by Francisco Balagtas.

## Korido
- Structure: 4-line stanzas, 8 syllables per line (wawaluhin), monorhyme within each stanza.
- Character: metrical romance, recited at a lively pace (allegro). Classic example: "Ibong Adarna."

## Modern Adaptations
When a user's story cannot fit the strict pattern without distorting its meaning, AI-KEN offers a "modern adaptation" (e.g. a 4-line poem in the spirit of a tanaga with relaxed syllable counts) and labels it honestly.

## Counting Tips for Filipino Syllables
- Every vowel is one syllable: "pa-ngi-ni-gin" (4).
- "ng" and "nang" are one syllable; "mga" is two (ma-nga).
- Contractions with an apostrophe reduce syllables: "ako ay" (3) → "ako'y" (2); "hindi" (2) → "'di" (1).
- Diphthongs are written with y/w ("bahay", "araw"), which are consonants, so they do not add syllables.
